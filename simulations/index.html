Simulations

Scientific simulations

Interactive models built on measured data, running entirely in your browser. Each one states the dataset it uses, the version of the model, what was reduced or approximated, and what it cannot tell you.

These are not videogames. They use game-like interaction — you can click, perturb and replay — but the point is the computation underneath, and a result you can trace back to its source. The games live in their own section.

Escape Reflex in 3D

The whole 167,106-neuron MaleCNS connectome drives a biomechanical Drosophila body in MuJoCo, seeing through 1,466 retinal columns by casting rays into the scene. A ball looms and the giant fibre fires. Remove the 185 LPLC2 cells and the response collapses; remove 311 neurons at random and it survives.

Dataset
MaleCNS v1.0, whole graph
Body
NeuroMechFly in MuJoCo
Vision
Optical, by ray casting
Runs
Recorded, 22× slower than real time

It does not fly, forage or steer, and the jump itself is not implemented — what is measured is the giant fibre’s firing rate. The page says so on the page.

Drosophila Connectome Laboratory

A named subcircuit of the MaleCNS Drosophila connectome: the neurons that lie on direct paths from the wind receptors of Johnston’s organ to the descending neurons for steering and locomotion. Set the wind direction, watch the circuit respond, remove neurons, and compare matched intact and lesioned runs.

Dataset
MaleCNS v1.0
Deployed
1,058 neurons · 58,753 connections
Computation
In-browser, Web Worker
Runs
Deterministic, self-tested

A subcircuit, not the fly brain — and no behaviour is simulated: there is no body and no room. The page says so, in full, on the page.
