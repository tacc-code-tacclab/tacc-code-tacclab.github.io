Simulations

Scientific simulations

Interactive models built on measured data, running entirely in your browser. Each one states the dataset it uses, the version of the model, what was reduced or approximated, and what it cannot tell you.

These are not videogames. They use game-like interaction — you can click, perturb and replay — but the point is the computation underneath, and a result you can trace back to its source. The games live in their own section.

Seven Experiments, Seven Failed Criteria

The rest of the work, including the parts that did not succeed. Seven experiments on the whole-CNS connectome, each with its pass/fail criteria written into the script before it ran and a size-matched random lesion as a control. Seven of the thirty-seven declared criteria failed, and those are the most informative results: a bitter veto that is real but not localisable, half of a classic dissociation, and no steering at all.

Dataset
MaleCNS v1.0, whole graph
Design
Criteria fixed before execution
Controls
Size-matched random lesions throughout
Page
Generated from the measurement files

Includes two mistakes of our own: an ablation call that silently fails to silence neurons driven by injected input, and an audit we nearly “corrected” on the strength of a single-timepoint reading.

Eaten or Not

The same machinery, scored on the outcome that matters: survival. A spider closes in from behind, aimed at the spot the fly is standing on, and does not correct its aim. Remove the 185 LPLC2 cells and the fly is caught. Remove 311 at random — more cells, blindly chosen, three independent draws — and it escapes every time.

Dataset
MaleCNS v1.0, whole graph
Outcome
Caught or escaped, by a declared rule
Controls
Three independent random draws
Runs
Six, recorded, identical starting state

Predation is not simulated: the spider has no collisions and does not strike. Capture is scored from the real positions by a criterion fixed in writing before any run, and reported at five values of the one free parameter.

Escape Reflex in 3D

The whole 167,106-neuron MaleCNS connectome drives a biomechanical Drosophila body in MuJoCo, seeing through 1,466 retinal columns by casting rays into the scene. A ball looms and the giant fibre fires. Remove the 185 LPLC2 cells and the response collapses; remove 311 neurons at random and it survives.

Dataset
MaleCNS v1.0, whole graph
Body
NeuroMechFly in MuJoCo
Vision
Optical, by ray casting
Runs
Recorded, 22× slower than real time

It does not fly, forage or steer. The connectome decides whether to jump; how fast and at what angle is a motor readout, labelled as such. The page says so on the page. For the same runs scored as survival, see Eaten or Not.

Drosophila Connectome Laboratory

A named subcircuit of the MaleCNS Drosophila connectome: the neurons that lie on direct paths from the wind receptors of Johnston’s organ to the descending neurons for steering and locomotion. Set the wind direction, watch the circuit respond, remove neurons, and compare matched intact and lesioned runs.

Dataset
MaleCNS v1.0
Deployed
1,058 neurons · 58,753 connections
Computation
In-browser, Web Worker
Runs
Deterministic, self-tested

A subcircuit, not the fly brain — and no behaviour is simulated: there is no body and no room. The page says so, in full, on the page.

Lunar Flight over Apollo 11

Fly over the Moon on terrain built from real NASA Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter data: LOLA elevation and LROC imagery for the whole globe, down to 2 m elevation and 0.5 m images around the Apollo 11 landing site. Pick a speed and survey from a few metres up to 3,000 km, with the official IAU names on craters, maria and rilles; or switch to lander physics and try to touch down. Works on phones.

Elevation
LOLA GDR · LROC NAC DTM
Imagery
LROC WAC · LROC NAC
Names
IAU / USGS gazetteer, 9,087 features
Runs
In-browser, Three.js

No procedural terrain and no texture synthesis: when you fly below the resolution of the data, the page says so instead of inventing detail. Fast flight simply moves the camera vehicle and is not physics; in lander mode the Moon’s rotation is neglected and propellant is unlimited.

Lunar Rover: prospecting on predicted ground

Start at a base station beside the Apollo 11 landing site. Fly a lander anywhere on the Moon, land, and drive a rover in lunar gravity: a detector ticks near thorium ore, the arm lifts and analyses rocks, the gun breaks the big ones, and every find becomes energy that makes the rover faster and stronger. The ground under the wheels is finer than any image of the Moon, so it is built in three layers the page can show in colour: measured, inferred from the shading of the real photograph, and predicted from lunar statistics.

Measured
LROC NAC DTM 2 m · photo 0.5 m
Inferred
Shape from shading, r = 0.70 vs DTM slopes
Predicted
Craters < 3 m, rocks < 1 m, statistics
Physics
1.62 m/s², no air, low-weight traction

The predicted layer is statistically plausible, not real: no individual small crater or rock exists as drawn, and its constants are quoted from memory and still to be checked against the papers. Ore as fuel, the gun and the lander’s speed are game elements. The journey to the Earth is not built yet. Not tested on a real phone.
