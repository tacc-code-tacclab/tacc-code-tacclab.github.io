What a connectome alone can and cannot do

The two other pages here show one experiment each, and both of them work. This page is the rest of the work, including the parts that did not. Seven experiments, each with its pass/fail criteria written into the script before it was executed, each with a size-matched random lesion as a control. Seven of the thirty-seven declared criteria failed, in three of the seven experiments. They are the most informative results on the page and they are reported first, not buried.

None of the ingredients are ours. The connectome is Janelia's, the neural model is flyverse-core, the body is NeuroMechFly in MuJoCo. What we added is an independent reproduction on our own hardware, experiments designed so that they could fail, and two mistakes of our own that are worth other people's time.

Two criteria failed, and they are the finding. T4/T5 direction selectivity, and the L1/L2 dissociation

2 criteri failed

Do the elementary motion detectors prefer the right directions, and does removing L1 or L2 dissociate the ON and OFF channels?

criteria, fixed before running
  1. intact: 8/8 preferred directions correct, minimum DSI ≥ 0.1
  2. L1 removed: T4 amplitude ≤ 30% of intact, T5 ≥ 50%
  3. L2 removed: T5 ≤ 30%, T4 ≥ 50%

The two amplitude criteria failed, and the random control explains why: removing 1,776 random rate units raised responses 2.6-fold, so amplitude is not comparable across arms. Read on direction instead — a ratio, immune to gain — L1 removal leaves 1 of 4 T4 subtypes correct while all 4 T5 subtypes are untouched. That is the ON half of the classic dissociation; the OFF half does not appear.

All three criteria failed. But one channel was tested with the wrong stimulus: the optomotor system answers to rotational optic flow, and the protocol never rotated the fly. Rotating it at 90°/s gives +7.28 Hz, close to the ~6 Hz the implementation's own notes anticipate. DNa02 meanwhile is not balanced but silent: R at 0.20 Hz with -1.67 mV of steady input and L at 0.00 Hz with -1.13 mV of steady input, against a 7.0 mV threshold gap.

Two mistakes of ours

Ablation does not silence a neuron driven by injected input

In the reference implementation, spikes are computed as (v ≥ threshold) × active and the injected Poisson input is then applied with a maximum — after the mask that implements ablation. A neuron driven by set_poisson therefore keeps firing when it has been "silenced". Measured: the 335 wind receptors nominally ablated fired at 100.65 Hz, exactly as intact, and sweet gustatory neurons nominally ablated still drove MN9 at 2.44 Hz.

Our first wind experiment therefore contained an arm that did not exist, and it read as a real negative finding. Anyone lesioning sensory receptors in this framework should silence the input at source and measure the firing rate of the cells they claim to have removed. Our scripts now raise if it has not fallen.

We nearly "corrected" a correct audit

A quick reading put DNa02 at +0.35 mV above rest, contradicting the reference implementation's own audit, which reports it held ~1.6–2.0 mV below rest. We were about to rewrite a published page on the strength of it. The audit was right: our reading took the membrane potential at a single instant after three seconds instead of averaging over a long run. Measured properly the two agree to within 0.3 mV. A single-timepoint reading is not a measurement, and a 400-line audit deserves more than one before being overturned.

Method, in brief

connectome
MaleCNS v1.0 (Janelia FlyEM, CC BY 4.0 at the time of writing). 167,106 compiled nodes, 25,578,600 edges, 124,161,872 synapses as loaded.
reproduction
The reference benchmark reproduced independently on our hardware before anything else: 42 of 42 population counts and 6 of 6 ledger categories matched, including values the reference itself marks as failures.
neural model
flyverse-core — spiking leaky integrate-and-fire with a receptor model over predicted neurotransmitter identity. Nothing trained, nothing fitted.
body
NeuroMechFly in MuJoCo, 48 leg actuators, adhesion, solver-resolved contacts, 0.1 ms physics step under a 10 ms control step.
vision
1,466 retinal column directions cast into the scene as seven rays each over the measured 4.5° acceptance angle — geometry, not supplied coordinates.
determinism
Synaptic depression off, so a repeat of any condition is bit-identical. The variation that matters is across random lesion draws, and three independent draws are used where the control is load-bearing.
what is not simulated
Flight, righting, steering, predation. Capture is scored geometrically from real positions; nothing is animated that the physics did not compute.

Where the margin runs out

One measurement deserves separating out, because it is a warning about this whole class of experiment. With a predator 16 mm across, the intact fly still escapes at 71.2 Hz — but all three size-matched random controls collapse to 25.2–27.6 Hz and none of them escape. In that configuration a targeted lesion would have "demonstrated" a specificity that was not there, because any lesion of that size breaks the response. The predator used on this page is the smallest one for which the control arms survive, and that choice was made by measuring the controls before looking at any result of interest.
