What this is, and what it is not

  • It is a named subcircuit, not the fly brain. The MaleCNS v1.0 connectome has 167,106 neurons and 25.6 million connections. This page holds the neurons that lie on direct paths from the Johnston's-organ wind receptors to the descending neurons for steering and locomotion — about a thousand of them. The reduction rule is stated in the provenance and is reproducible from the export script.
  • The wiring is measured; the dynamics are a declared reduction. The weights are the ones the reference implementation ships, with its receptor model applied (md5 in the provenance). The dynamics here are a first-order rate model, not the spiking LIF of the reference implementation. Same graph, different and simpler dynamics.
  • It does not predict what a fly would do. No behaviour is simulated: there is no body, no room and no locomotion on this page. What you change is the firing of a circuit, and what you read is the firing of its output neurons.
  • The recurrent gain is a chosen parameter. Row-normalised recurrence with gain 0.8, picked by a declared sweep (no saturation, under half the cells silent, peak rate in a biological range). The sign of the descending asymmetry holds across the whole admissible range, so it is not an artefact of that choice.
  • Nothing is hidden when it fails. If the numerics diverge the page stops and says so. There is no fallback animation.
